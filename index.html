🎯 The One-Sentence Diagnosis

This is an occupancy problem, not a pricing problem — our rate is above the comp set while our occupancy runs 8.6 points below it. The fix is service, cleanliness, and reviews.

Therefore: do not cut rates. Cutting rate on a property that already sells above the comp set would surrender the one index we are winning (ARI 102) without addressing the one we are losing (MPI 89).

📌 Key Facts — On-Site Audit & Data Review
Outdoor fireplace is unique — no competitor in Pooler has one, and it appears nowhere in our marketing or OTA photos. Zero-cost asset sitting idle.
100's Grill food quality is genuinely good — better than most competitors. It is not free, which is a perception problem, not a quality problem.
Guest mix: 60%+ are 56 or older, ~68% leisure travelers. Amenity and accessibility decisions should be aimed at this guest, not a generic business traveler.

💡 Strategic Insights
The outdoor fireplace is our best untapped asset. No competitor has one. Fix the OTA listing photos and name it at check-in. Zero cost, this week.
An evening amenity beats free breakfast. Drury's Kickback drives more reviews than its breakfast does. A drink at the bar 5–7pm mirrors it, works with the breakfast constraint, and is cheaper than giving away 104 breakfasts.
Guest laundry is a real gap. Hampton and Courtyard have it. Port workers and contractors on 3+ night stays need it — the exact segment where our scores are worst.
Our guest is older than our amenity strategy assumes. 60%+ are 56 or older and ~68% travel for leisure. Lighting, bathroom grab-safety, legible signage and unhurried check-in matter more here than a co-working nook.
Fairfield Marriott is the biggest threat. New, 4.8★, free breakfast, Bonvoy. Review velocity is the metric to watch, not their current star rating.
Counter Courtyard with warmth, not features. We will not out-spend a Starbucks partnership. Food quality, the fireplace, and a check-in that remembers you are the winnable ground.

📐 Caveats Kept Explicit
Company names are OCR-read. The top 50 were verified against the scans; 799 were double-read at 300 and 600 dpi; 3 are flagged CHECK SPELLING in the workbook. Numbers are unaffected — accounts group by Corporate ID, not by name — but verify a name before putting it in a deliverable or in front of the GM.
Merging duplicate profiles is a documented judgement, not a fact from the report. The basis is recorded per row in the workbook's "Merged on" column and the unmerged view is kept intact. Two similar pairs were deliberately not merged: Al Farm Bureau vs Ga Farm Bureau (Alabama vs Georgia), and AMN Healthcare Services vs Healthcare Services.
Active / lapsed / declining / revenue-at-risk are our definitions, not the report's. Active = booked in 2025 or 2026 YTD. Lapsed = produced in 2023–24 and nothing since. Growing and declining = a ±15% band on 2025–26 versus 2023–24.
Method lesson worth keeping. The extraction looked clean and complete at four separate points while it was silently dropping ~75 accounts, flipping the sign on refunds, passing a cell with two compensating errors, and undercounting duplicate profiles. Every one of those was caught by an independent check against the report's own printed totals — never by the pipeline agreeing with itself. Internal consistency is not evidence of correctness.

⚙️ How This System Works

This hub covers the Holiday Inn & Suites Savannah Airport – Pooler, 100 Outlet Pkwy, Pooler GA 31322 only. All data here is private and confidential to this project and should never be shared outside the hotel team.

1
Presenting? Open the deck, not the dashboard.
Open presentation.html — 10 slides, arrow keys or space to advance, F for fullscreen, P to print to PDF. The dashboard is your backup for questions: if your dad asks something specific, switch to the Diagnosis or Accounts tab and show him the underlying number.
2
Tabs, in the order the story runs
Home is the summary. Diagnosis is the proof — occupancy chart, the three indices, the money, the guest scores. The Plan is the 7 initiatives and the 90 days. Intel, Outreach and Accounts are the supporting detail.
3
After every visit — paste your raw notes to Claude
No formatting needed. Just paste and say "save this." The dashboard, memory files, and blueprint all get updated together.
"Here are my notes from today: [paste]"
4
New STR report → new chart
Upload a fresh STAR PDF and ask Claude to refresh the Diagnosis tab. The occupancy chart, the three indices, and the money figures all recalculate from it.
5
Everything works offline
Both files are fully self-contained — no internet needed to open them, and the charts are drawn by the file itself. That is deliberate: a presentation should not depend on someone's conference-room wifi.
6
Wednesday 9am — the rating monitor runs
Checks competitor rankings and new reviews for our property automatically.
📐 Data Integrity Rules
Hard data: Heartbeat, FPS, Medallia, and STR STAR. These are cited as fact.
Labeled assumptions: all cost estimates. Never presented as quotes.
Never used: Clearwater (CLWHS) screenshots — that is a different property.
Never shared externally: any internal performance data on this dashboard.
Authority: Shyam is an intern. Outreach opens relationships; it never commits the hotel to a rate or a billing arrangement.
